## What was built

- **RFC** [`BITCOIN-TREASURY.md`](../../BITCOIN-TREASURY.md): three options compared, provider facts with sources, an operating model, questions for counsel and a CPA, a threat model. Bitcoin stays optional and outside JRCv1.
- **qmt 0.1** ([spec](../../spec/quartermaster/QMT-JOURNAL.md), [schema](../../spec/quartermaster/qmt-0.1.schema.json)) and `lib/treasury/`: a hash-chained double-entry journal, evidence classes, offline Bitcoin proofs, custody tracking, FIFO lots, RFC 6962 snapshots, salted commitments.
- **Read-only adapters:** Apple Financial reports, bank CSV profiles, Kraken ledgers, BTCPay Greenfield, Esplora.
- **Interfaces:** the `qm treasury` CLI, and `/treasury/` with an in-browser check.
- **Simulated Apple-to-Bitcoin example** in regtest blocks, regenerated byte-for-byte.

## Decisions

- **Option B as the baseline, C optional.** A committed, timestamped ledger delivers "history can't be quietly rewritten" without holding a volatile asset or taking on new legal duties. Bitcoin proves only the coins themselves.
- **Integers and BigInt everywhere money is.** Canonical JSON refuses floats, so a hash never depends on number formatting.
- **No key handling at all.** Nothing in the code can sign or broadcast. Simulated treasury scripts hash a public phrase, so no private key exists for them.
- **"Chain-verified" needs the viewer's own anchor.** Offline proof checks plus a block hash the viewer confirmed. Regtest can't pass as mainnet because of the difficulty-limit check.
- **The example runs on synthetic regtest blocks rather than mainnet transactions.** JRC has no treasury, and pointing at someone else's real transactions would be misleading. Real mainnet data (block 170, genesis) is used only as test vectors.
- **Apple refunds are netted into the period's entry by default.** A lone refund row was both withheld and inferable from neighbouring records.
- **Raw research notes aren't published.** Some quote provider pages at length. The RFC carries the findings in its own words with links.

## Reviews and fixes

All reviewers worked from `git archive` copies of commit bf44014 and reproduced each finding with a script. They are agents working for the project, not independent auditors. Each fix below has a regression test in `test/treasury.test.mjs` (the `review (...)` tests).

**Privacy** (most serious):
- **Balances leaked withheld amounts.** Snapshot balances and summaries were computed from the full journal, so withheld records could be recovered by subtraction (e.g. the $50 donation; the $9.99 refund). Every summary is now computed from public records only.
- **Unsalted aggregates.** An aggregate below the threshold was unsalted and brute-forced in under a second. All withholdable records are now salted; weak or reused salts are refused.
- **Withheld details on the page.** Withheld rows showed on the page by month and kind. They now appear only as leaf hashes.
- **Links to withheld records.** Public records could name withheld ones (`follows`). Publication is now refused in that case, and when a withheld record has a chain proof.
- **Scanner gaps.**
  - A field-name exemption let card numbers through.
  - IBANs, ABA numbers, "acct no.", INV-numbers, SSN/EIN, phone numbers and xpubs were missed.
  - Object keys weren't scanned.

  All fixed.
- **Raw memos and refids.** Raw bank memos could be copied into public entries, and Kraken refids became ids. Both stopped.
- **Address reuse.** The demo reused an address. It no longer does.
- **Unlabelled files.** Some simulated source files weren't labelled. They are now.
- **The honest limit.** A withheld record's existence still shows, and its size can sometimes be inferred. In the example the bank looks overdrawn by $20.51. The RFC, spec and page now say so.

**Accounting:**
- **Transfers and conversions.** These could post to expense accounts. Now only positive fees are allowed, and mixed-commodity legs must be asset, conversion or fee accounts.
- **Sell-side conversions.** These gave a 0.00 rate and no proceeds.
- **Foreign-currency purchases.** A purchase in a non-base currency got basis 0. It's now unknown.
- **FIFO order.** FIFO followed journal order, not date.
- **Pending entries.**
  - A posted reversal could reverse a pending entry.
  - Pending estimates couldn't be retired. They now can, with `retires`.
  - Reversed conversions still counted.
- **Apple refunds.** The refund sign depended on Apple's Customer Price sign. A mismatched row was still booked, and the CLI hid parse issues.
- **BTCPay valuation.** Income was valued at a caller-chosen price. It's now valued at BTCPay's own rate.
- **Edge cases.**
  - Double-precision balancing let unbalanced entries through.
  - Prices of any date could value an entry.
  - Conversion accounts could park other entries.
  - "12,50" was read as $1,250.
  - Pending future entries moved `as_of`.

**Reproducibility:**
- **Anchors.** `anchors` output couldn't feed `--anchors`.
- **Timestamp digest.** `ots stamp snapshot.json` would have stamped a different digest from the published one, because of a trailing newline.
- **Line endings.** A CRLF checkout failed opaquely. `.gitattributes` now pins the files, and `verify` explains the failure.
- **`chain-check`.** It had no ready input and no `--require-anchor`.
- **Missing CLI pieces.** Trades couldn't be imported from the CLI. The rules format was undocumented.
- **Canonical JSON.** Key ordering and NFC rules were unspecified. The spec now has them, with test vectors.

**Legal and claims.** No blocking issues: no claim of adoption, certification or holdings, and Option C was presented as optional. Corrected:
- **Fees on self-transfers:** the RFC now states the IRS position that they are dispositions, consistently with the code.
- **State adoption:** the count of states using the model act was overstated.
- **Form 8282:** the framing was loose.
- **Provider facts:** several were stated more firmly than their sources (BTCPay sizing, rate lock, River holds, Apple refund conventions).
- **OFAC:** the citations were imprecise.
- **ASU 2023-08:** its scope was overstated.
- **Rewrite threat:** the residual risk was overstated.

**Transaction security.** The primitives held: no Merkle, SPV, transaction-parsing or address forgery was found. The accounting layer around them broke:
- **Failed proofs counted as custody.** A proof that failed offline (all-zero header, empty branch) still counted toward the "proven" balance. Proofs must now check offline to enter the journal.
- **Phantom coins.**
  - Reusing a proof resurrected spent coins.
  - Booking a self-send's change as a donation created coins.
  - Two accounts sharing an address were both credited.

  Now each txid has one proof, each proof supports one entry, each address belongs to one account, and booked on-chain balances must equal the coins the proofs show.
- **`verify` didn't recompute figures.** It checked only the root and counts, so hand-edited balances and evidence counts passed. It now recomputes the whole snapshot from the public records and compares bytes.
- **Unanchored proofs were forgeable.** The reviewer mined a minimum-difficulty mainnet header for a made-up 1 BTC transaction in about six minutes. Unanchored proofs now never count as proven, and the class description says why.
- **Prototype names.** Names such as `constructor` or `__proto__` broke lookups. The lookup tables now have no prototype, and those ids are refused.
- **Contradicted outflows.** An outflow the chain disagreed with kept the `chain-verified` label. It is now `contradicted`.
- **Appends and commitments.** `append-check` accepted invalid appended records, and commitments could carry extra fields.
- **32-bit wrap.** Inclusion proofs accepted index and size values past 2^32 (int32 wrap), and disclosures weren't bound to the record's position.
- **Smaller fixes:**
  - The same explorer written two ways counted as two anchor sources.
  - Testnet URLs were wrong, and regtest could query a mainnet explorer.
  - Explorer responses were buffered before the size check, and had no timeout.
  - Explorer Merkle data wasn't validated.
  - Non-integer block heights were accepted, and test networks could mix with mainnet in one journal.
  - Segwit encodings with all-empty witnesses were accepted.
  - The BTCPay client dropped a sub-path in its URL.

Before fixing, the three most serious attacks (failed proof counted as custody; forged snapshot figures passing `verify`; the withheld $50 readable from the balances) were re-run against commit bf44014 and succeeded. The regression tests show each is now refused.

## Mistakes

- The first version of the snapshot and page leaked withheld records in exactly the way the RFC said they wouldn't. The privacy reviewer caught it.
- Several first-run test failures were the agent's own wrong expectations, not code faults. They were corrected after checking the code was right, for example that a redacted journal keeps the private journal's leaf hashes.

## Not done

- **No real treasury, keys, accounts or providers.**
- **No live BTCPay, Apple or exchange API calls.** Those clients are tested against stubbed transports.
- **Formats not handled:** Lightning, FX gains and losses, OFX/CAMT.053, and a River profile (headers unconfirmed).
- **No consistency proofs between snapshots,** and no plain-text accounting export.
- **No MCP/WebMCP tools for the treasury yet.**
- **No real-browser check of `/treasury/`.**
- **No OpenTimestamps proof for the example snapshot.** It's simulated, and stamping is a human step.
