# Historical Narrative, Verified Quotations and Creative Direction

## Quotes explicitly retained by request

### Thomas Jefferson, letter to Isaac McPherson, August 13, 1813

> “He who receives an idea from me, receives instruction himself without lessening mine”

Verified through Monticello's transcription of Jefferson's letter; the passage reflects the non-rivalrous nature of sharing ideas. **Do not attribute to Jefferson a blanket opposition to every modern copyright protection; contextualize the 1813 letter, which principally addresses invention/patent questions.** Original letters have different spellings and punctuation from common normalized editions.

Source: https://tjrs.monticello.org/letter/293

### Steve Wozniak, 1984 Hackers Conference, as recounted by Steven Levy

> “Information should be free but your time should not.”

Steven Levy recounts this response to Stewart Brand at the 1984 Hackers Conference, in a 2014 retrospective. This connects shared information and the economic reality of human labor without confusing them.

Source: https://www.wired.com/story/hackers-at-30-hackers-and-information-wants-to-be-free/

## Historical lineage

- Scientific, artistic and hacker cultures of copying, learning and remixing.
- Jefferson's correspondence on the nature of sharing ideas.
- Early programming and hacker communities, with sharing and paid labor coexisting uneasily.
- GNU/free software and its four freedoms, followed by open source, copyleft, public-domain projects and permissive commercial reuse.
- Personal data portability, user sovereignty and decentralized identities, including AT Protocol.
- Reproducible builds, infrastructure-as-code, transparent FinOps and agentic software engineering.
- Proposed synthesis: operational transparency and the freedom to continue.

Do not claim no one has attempted similar work. Precedents include GNU, SQLite, Ghost, free culture, Open Source Maintenance Fee, Spec Kit, atproto portability, and FinOps projects. See `research/PRECEDENTS.md`.

## Three communications formats

**Headline:** Free. Libre. Truly Free.

**Short explanation:** We don't just share source. We share how it was built, how to operate it, what it costs and how to keep user data portable. Anyone can reproduce, maintain, improve or sell the software, including our competitors.

**Manifesto hook:** Every creative work owes something to those who came before it. If we can reproduce code with agents, we should also be able to reproduce its process, infrastructure, economics and stewardship. Freedom is the ability to continue without permission.

## Design direction, not an approved brand

Pirate flag, naval charts, old proclamations, woodcut and printing-press imagery can evoke anti-monopoly history without implying actual lawlessness. Balance radical storytelling with restrained, credible engineering documentation. Naming and graphic rights must be reviewed before public use.
