# Organic Launch and AI-Led Communications Plan (No Paid Media)

> **Partly superseded (2026-10-20):** the founding-provenance work it mentions was retired on 2026-10-19. See [`SHARE-READINESS.md`](SHARE-READINESS.md) for the current announcement checklist.

**Goal:** motivate independent examination and participation, not just clicks. Public engagement should be earned through useful evidence, honest debate and transparent execution. Advertising budget: **$0**. Domain registration and approved essential operating expenses are separately recorded in open costs.

## Narrative architecture

- **5 seconds:** "You can't pirate what belongs to everyone."
- **30 seconds:** "The code is free. The development process is reproducible. The infrastructure and costs are public. Your identity and data remain portable. Anyone can compete to maintain the software."
- **Technical thesis:** "Existing software freedoms are necessary but insufficient when code is reproducible yet the build process, production infrastructure, billing and user data are inaccessible. JRCv1 proposes independently verifiable lifecycle freedom."
- **Flagship public challenge:** "Could your software survive without you?"
- **Evidence:** Complete open build documentation, real cost records, a successful third-party reproduction and documented disagreement.

## Distribution order (each with original, useful contribution)

1. **GitHub first:** source documents, Discussions, RFCs, issue templates, open dependencies and starter kit. The repository must stand alone as proof of transparency.
2. **Personal launch essay on LinkedIn:** candid origin story: collaboration with ChatGPT, subsequent Claude-led build, invitation to break the model. Include 2–3 clear ways to contribute. Human reviews and posts from their account.
3. **Bluesky/AT Protocol:** distributed announcement thread, open Lexicon discussion, reposts only from real consenting people; add decentralized discovery later.
4. **Developer community articles:** DEV, personal blog and Mastodon/Fosstodon, each with engineering substance, documented architecture and source rather than marketing-only reposts.
5. **Hacker News:** a human-authored thoughtful technical submission once there is an inspectable implementation. Avoid bots, scripted comments, coordinated voting and promotional manipulation. A "Show HN" post requires a usable project, not only a manifesto.
6. **Reddit/other forums:** only if participation follows the specific community's rules; be transparent about project affiliation and engage with disagreement.
7. **Press/newsletters/podcasts:** voluntary outreach only after a verifiable story (e.g., outside reproduction, verified savings, third-party adoption). Ask for critique, never pretend an endorsement.
8. **Local/remote meetups:** open, editable slide deck and 2-minute talk with historical quotations and precise sources. Anyone may adapt it.

## Opening campaign arcs

**Arc A: The provocation** — One line and explanation: "You can't pirate what belongs to everyone." Clarify authorized copying vs. piracy in violation of someone else's rights.

**Arc B: The historical lineage** — Brief, sourced Jefferson idea-sharing line and Wozniak's “Information should be free but your time should not”; acknowledge GNU/FSF, SQLite, CC0 and relevant modern projects. Do not invent being the first.

**Arc C: The challenge** — "Could your software survive without you?" Invite real demos of independent reproduction and publicly document failure modes.

**Arc D: Show the receipts** — Actual infrastructure, real costs, imputed human attention separately, provenance and decision logs. Never show fabricated numbers as bills.

**Arc E: Let the community break it** — Launch `What Would Make This Fail?` and publish principled objections and resulting revisions.

## First 30 days: editorial plan

| Period | Public artifact | Community request |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Founding charter, source attribution, provenance, initial website | Find contradictions; debate mission |
| Week 2 | Full build/deploy guide and initial independence challenge | Try clean third-party build |
| Week 3 | Quartermaster cost-schema architecture and public RFC | Suggest data sources and cost methodology |
| Week 4 | Documented results: criticism received, implemented improvements, cost ledger, blockers | Volunteer for a maintainable workstream |

Daily social posts are not a target. A smaller number of high-quality, useful posts should be preferred. Publish editorial backlog and factual measurements, but avoid metrics that reward compulsive attention.

## AI-led operations, human-accountable publishing

- AI can research claims, verify historical sources, draft copy, propose images, translate drafts, compare channel norms, plan schedules, generate site pages and draft replies.
- AI must keep an editorial issue/PR for each public campaign and link to the evidence it cites. One or more reviewers approve external public publication.
- Do not autonomously interact with people using undisclosed bot personas; do not send bulk direct messages, buy engagement, scrape restricted sites or evade automated moderation.
- Use relevant platform APIs only with proper authorization and within their terms. If posting isn't permitted, prepare a copy-ready draft.
- Publicly disclose significant AI assistance. No fake human-only authorship; no invented testimonials or performance claims.
- Establish an always-available but opt-in newsletter/RSS (RSS first, email only when consent/compliance exists). No dark patterns.

## Immediate starter materials

- Website hero and 30-second explanation.
- One-page Charter PDF/Markdown (Markdown is canonical), source-linked historical quote cards and attribution.
- `What Would Make This Fail?` invitation text and `RFC` template.
- The challenge checklist and a reproducible site build demonstration.
- Accessible copyable introduction for independent advocates; organizers may adapt it without asking permission.

## Measurements that don't reward wasted attention

Track public, verifiable outcomes: external PRs, independent adopters, successful cold reproductions, accepted and rejected RFCs, community maintainers, real cost savings, testable data migrations, and founder-absence contributions. Follower counts and pageviews may be contextual but not the success definition. Publish unknown metrics as unknown.

## Source/rights checks

Keep historical quotes brief and cite their original sources; confirm relevant publication rights for any image. Avoid generalized claims of legally new invention or universal copyright abolition. Confirm organizational/brand availability and naming rights before requesting press coverage.
